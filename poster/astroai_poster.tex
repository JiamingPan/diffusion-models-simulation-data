\begin{tcolorbox}[secbox={paramBlue}{The question}]
\SmallFont
\begin{minipage}[t]{14.6cm}
\vspace{0pt}
Diffusion models are attractive as fast priors for cosmological fields, but
inference needs more than visual realism: samples must be new, conditioned, and
statistically faithful. On CAMELS HI maps we ask:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=9mm, itemsep=1.8mm, label=\textcolor{paramBlue}{\textbf{\arabic*.}}]
\item \hl{loopRed}{Memorization:} when do generated maps stop matching
      individual training slices?
\item \hl{diffPurple}{Conditioning:} does changing
      $\bm{\theta}_{\rm ask}$ change the recovered cosmology?
\item \hl{fieldTeal}{Fidelity:} do novel maps retain the one-point PDF and
      power spectrum?
\end{enumerate}

Toy picture: if the learned prior covers only training islands, the posterior
can be sharp but displaced from the true distribution.
\end{minipage}\hfill

\vspace{0pt}

\end{minipage}

\begin{tcolorbox}[secbox={diffPurple}{Data \& models}]
\SmallFont
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=11mm, itemsep=2.2mm, label=\textcolor{diffPurple}{$\blacktriangleright$}]
\item \textbf{CAMELS} (IllustrisTNG) LH set: 1000 simulations $\to$ 128
      slices each $\to$ 128{,}000 candidate $128\times128$ HI maps.
\item Six-parameter conditioning:
      $\bm{\theta}=(\Omega_m,\sigma_8,A_{\mathrm{SN1}},A_{\mathrm{AGN1}},
      A_{\mathrm{SN2}},A_{\mathrm{AGN2}})$.
\item \textbf{Diffusion objective.} We corrupt a real map with noise and
      train a UNet to predict the velocity target at every noise level,
      with the cosmology $\bm{\theta}$ as conditioning.

\vspace{-1mm}
\begingroup
\fontsize{30}{36}\selectfont
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_t &= \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon,
\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),\\[1mm]
\mathcal{L}_{\rm diff}
&=\mathbb{E}_{x_0,\epsilon,t}\left\|
f_{\theta}(x_t,t,\bm{\theta})-\mathrm{target}\right\|_2^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
\endgroup
{\TinyPosterFont\color{subgray}
Here the config uses \texttt{v\_prediction}:
$\mathrm{target}=v_t=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon-\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0$.
Ho et al. 2020 (DDPM).}
\item \textbf{Sampling}: 50-step DPM-Solver, $8\times$ faster than the
      500-step DDPM baseline at matched quality.
\item \textbf{Training-set sweep}: $N_{2D}=2^{6} \to 2^{15}$ fields, matched
      optimizer-update budget; runs on U-M Great Lakes HPC.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[secbox={fieldTeal}{Detecting memorization}]
\SmallFont
For each generated map $x_j$, compute its similarity to every training map
$y_i$ and keep the closest match:
\begingroup
\fontsize{35}{43}\selectfont
\[
s_j = \max_i\, \mathrm{sim}(x_j,\, y_i)
\]
\endgroup
A high $s_j$ means $x_j$ is nearly a copy of one training map. We measure
$\mathrm{sim}$ in pixel, PCA, and SSCD space. Two independently trained
models should agree statistically while no individual map repeats; repeated
maps mean both copied the training set.

  {generated / nearest-train visual}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[secbox={diffPurple}{Memorization $\to$ generalization transition}]
\figslot{26.0cm}{12.4cm}{nf_generalize_fig2_poster_pca_generalization_q95_serif_clean.png}
  {quickcheck notebook $\to$ PCA generalization curves with q95 threshold}

\vspace{1mm}
The generalization score is the fraction of generated maps that are
\emph{not} close to any training map,
\begin{center}
\vspace{1mm}
\scalebox{1.7}{$\mathrm{GL} = \Pr[\, s_j < \tau \,]$\,,}
\vspace{1mm}
\end{center}
with $\tau$ the 95th percentile of train-vs-train closest-match similarity
(PCA space).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=11mm, itemsep=2mm, label=\textcolor{diffPurple}{$\blacktriangleright$}]
\item $\mathrm{GL} \approx 0$: almost every generated map sits on top of a
      training map. The model is \hl{loopRed}{copying}.
\item $\mathrm{GL} \approx 1$: generated maps are no closer to training
      than training maps are to each other; the model makes
      \hl{fieldTeal}{new fields}.
\item The jump happens over a narrow range of $N_{2D}$; the wider UNet-256
      needs more data {\color{subgray}(Zhang et al.\ 2024; Kadkhodaie et
      al.\ 2024; Yoon et al.\ 2023)}.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\hl{vggOrange}{Main result:} the bars show 16--84\% seed scatter at fixed

the requested $\Omega_m$ within that scatter. In the memorization regime, seeds

\begin{tcolorbox}[secbox={loopRed}{Takeaways}]
\SmallFont
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=12mm, itemsep=2mm, label=\textcolor{loopRed}{\textbf{\arabic*.}}]
\item \textbf{The transition appears in cosmological data.} The
      memorization-to-generalization transition known from natural images
      also occurs in CAMELS HI maps.
\item \textbf{It explains the error bars.} Small training sets give
      near-copies: inference looks precise but is biased toward the training
      values. Past the transition, $\bm{\theta}$ follows the input with
      honest scatter.
\item \textbf{It gives a practical check.} Nearest-neighbor similarity and
      encoder calibration reveal an emulator's regime before inference.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
